Thoroughly inspired by [@0xSero](https://x.com/0xSero), `Hot Cross Buns` was the first project where I thoroughly owned, iterated on and agonised over every layer of the project. Between owning the systems *(model steering, backend, fault tolerance, optimization)*, product *(UI/UX and frontend)*, and infra *(deployment, observability)*, I've gained so much from working on `Hot Cross Buns` and I'll always be grateful for the opportunity.

My main takeaway from building it out was that thinking about software is the easy part (probably only about 35%) of the overall work. The real work is talking to users, getting foraml *(and informal)* feedback, and working towards something people will actually use. Nothing is quite as humbling as watching a real person stumble around your app unguided, but I guess that's what it takes to build software for humans.

A secondary takeaway is also that [dogfooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eating_your_own_dog_food) is really, really important.
